## CH1
0: Night one.
1: The store intercom says your name.
2: You are the only person in the building.
3: It is seven past three in the morning, and you are shelving tins in aisle 5.
4: Then the front doors slide open by themselves.
5: A man is standing there, a regular, here every night at three.
6: Tonight he does not buy his paper and his coffee.
7: He just stands and stares, one fist clenched around something small.
8: The doors keep trying to close on him, and he keeps blocking them.
9: Rhea, the night manager, drives him back outside with a shopping cart and turns the lock.
10: He bumps the glass, steps back, and bumps it again.
11: Up in the office, twelve cameras show the town.
12: On every screen, someone is running.
--
13: Rhea grabs her keys and her coat.
14: She presses a sealed envelope into your hand.
15: Do not open it before day ten, she says.
16: And not even then, unless you have nothing left to try.
17: Then she is gone, and the doors lock behind her.
18: It is the last human voice you will hear for twelve days.
19: Or so you think.
--
20: You are alone with 25,000 products and one wall of glass.
21: Problem one: the glass.
22: There is a steel roller shutter, and it needs mains power.
23: If the power dies first, you wind it down by hand.
24: So you wind it down.
25: It takes 35 seconds, and it roars like a freight train.
26: House rule one: nobody comes in.
27: Outside, the regular is still pressed against the steel.
28: In his fist, you finally see it: a small brass key, tagged with a number.
29: Locker twelve.
--
30: At 5:23, the power goes out.
31: The freezers fall silent.
32: From the back of the store comes a heavy clunk.
33: The fire exit is held shut by a magnet, and magnets need power.
34: You sprint the whole length of the store.
35: You get there too late.
36: The door is open, and wet footprints lead inside.
--
## CH2
0: You slam the fire exit and drag four pallets of cat litter against it.
1: Then you lock every door and snap your own key off in the staff entrance.
2: Now nobody can get in.
3: You tell yourself that is the same as nobody being inside.
--
4: The footprints dry up by the bakery.
5: You search every aisle with a flashlight from aisle 9.
6: Nothing.
7: Emergency lights are built to last two hours, long enough for everyone to leave.
8: Nobody planned for somebody staying.
9: When they die, you learn something about supermarkets.
10: They have no windows.
11: With the shutter down, you have built yourself a cave.
--
12: Problem two: the rot.
13: Without power, a fridge keeps food safe for five hours.
14: A full freezer, three days.
15: After that, you are sealed in with three tons of meat, milk and fish.
16: But there is a walk-in freezer, a steel room with a rubber seal.
17: It keeps the warm air out, and everything else in.
18: You spend nine hours and one cart filling it.
19: You keep the bread and the fruit.
20: Then you tape the seams and write on the door: do not open.
21: Ever.
--
22: On the second morning, something is wrong.
23: The tins you stacked in aisle 5 have been rebuilt into a pyramid.
24: You did not build a pyramid.
25: You check the cameras.
26: They have no power.
27: You check the locks.
28: Every one is still shut.
29: Somebody is living in this building with you.
--
30: On the third night, you hold Rhea's envelope up to a flashlight.
31: You can almost read the words through the paper.
32: You slide it back into your apron.
33: Day ten, she said.
34: Not before.
--
35: On the fourth morning, you climb onto the roof.
36: Three nights ago, the parking lot was empty.
37: Now there are 150 people standing in it, all facing the doors.
38: They are not hunting you.
39: They are waiting for the store to open.
--
## CH3
0: Here is what the dead do.
1: They do not hunt.
2: They repeat.
3: They arrive around seven.
4: They lean on the shutter all day.
5: But when the sun goes down and the air turns cold, they stop.
6: Not drift off.
7: Stop.
8: They stand in the lot like statues until morning.
9: You do not know why.
10: You write it on the wall anyway.
--
11: So you work at night, and you live on the roof.
12: You cook on a camping stove, never indoors.
13: In a sealed building, a gas flame will kill you more quietly than they ever could.
--
14: On day four, the taps run dry.
15: You have eight pallets of bottled water, so drinking is easy.
16: The toilets are the problem.
17: One bucket, one bin bag, and a trip to aisle 13 for cat litter.
--
18: On day five, Rhea's van pulls into the staff lot.
19: It stops perfectly between the lines.
20: The engine runs until the tank is empty.
21: When you finally climb down, the driver's door is open and the seat is empty.
22: On the dashboard sits a child's drawing of a house.
23: Somebody has smashed the passenger window from the inside.
--
24: Every night at 9:00, you hear it.
25: Three knocks, a pause, three more.
26: It is not coming from the doors.
27: It is coming from the walk-in freezer.
--
28: Every night at nine, you stand with your hand an inch from the handle.
29: Every night, you take it back.
30: Rule two: that door opens only when there is no other way.
--
31: On day nine, you are asleep on a pile of dog beds when the ceiling turns white.
32: Every light blazes and every freezer roars.
33: And from the speakers, the intercom wakes up.
34: Attention shoppers, says a voice.
35: The store is now closing.
36: It says your name.
37: Outside, 250 heads turn at once.
--
## CH4
0: The main breaker is in the back office, seventy yards away.
1: You run it faster than you have ever run anything.
2: You are still ten steps short when the lights die on their own.
3: Thirty-five seconds of light, then darkness, then silence.
4: You did not touch the breaker.
5: Somebody else did.
--
6: You sit on the office floor with Rhea's envelope.
7: It is day ten.
8: You swore to open it only as a last resort.
9: The power is gone, the crowd is gathering, and you are out of ideas.
10: This is the last resort.
11: You tear it open.
12: Inside, there is no letter.
13: Only a floor plan with one thing circled: a service tunnel under the freezer.
14: And one line in her handwriting: Do not let them hear the cold.
--
15: You do not understand it yet.
16: You understand the crowd, though.
17: They are not leaning anymore.
18: They are pushing.
19: Problem three: the shutter.
20: It was built to stop one man with a crowbar.
21: Not 250 people.
22: By midnight, the steel has started to bow.
--
23: So you build a second wall.
24: Water pallets, a ton each, against the steel.
25: Shopping carts, nested and chained, thirty to a row.
26: But a wall only buys time.
27: You need them to leave.
--
28: Rhea's van is still in the lot.
29: In the staff locker room, you try the brass key from the regular's fist.
30: Locker twelve is Rhea's.
31: Inside: her phone, a photograph of two kids, and the van's remote.
32: You climb to the roof, aim the remote, and hold the panic button.
33: Down in the lot, the horn starts screaming.
34: Two hundred and fifty heads swing the other way.
--
## CH5
0: It buys you two days.
1: Then, on the twelfth night, a rope drops from the roof of the building next door.
2: Three people slide down it in white protective suits.
3: They carry a clipboard, a thermometer, and a very simple plan.
4: They know about the freezer.
5: They have been tracking it for days.
--
6: Problem four: the living.
7: You cannot fight three people with suits and tools.
8: But you understand something they do not.
9: If they cut the shutter, 250 dead walk in behind them.
10: And then nobody has a supermarket.
--
11: So you do not open the door.
12: You open the store.
13: One rope, one bucket, lowered off the back of the roof at 1:30 in the morning.
14: The first fifteen tins are free.
15: After that, you trade: batteries, fuel, medicine, news.
16: You worked retail for five years.
17: You know how to handle difficult customers.
18: House rule one, amended: nobody comes in, but the roof is open.
--
19: By day sixteen, there is a queue.
20: A woman with a baby, two old men with a wheelbarrow, and a girl on a bike who only ever asks for cat food.
21: On the seventeenth night, you finally catch the thing that has been moving your tins.
22: It is a small girl, nine years old, living in the ceiling.
23: Her name is Mina.
24: She has been up there since before the lights went out.
25: In her backpack, one enormous orange cat.
--
26: On day twenty, the bucket comes up with a note in it.
27: The people in white suits are done trading.
28: They are coming tonight.
29: They do not want the food.
30: They want whatever is behind the door that says do not open.
--
## CH6
0: At 9:00 that night, the knocking starts.
1: Three knocks, a pause, three more.
2: This time, someone knocks back from the other side of the roof hatch.
3: You look at Mina.
4: Mina looks at you.
5: You finally say it out loud: there is somebody in the freezer.
--
6: Why do the dead go still at night?
7: Because the cold slows them down.
8: Why do they stay still in the freezer?
9: Because Rhea went in there on night one.
10: She was bitten at the fire exit.
11: She locked herself in the one place that would keep her human.
12: The envelope was not a map for escaping.
13: It was a map to reach her.
--
14: Then you prepare for the suits.
15: You cannot stop three people.
16: But you know what can.
17: Thirty alarm clocks from aisle 9, and the horn on the van.
18: Winding them takes one person thirty minutes.
19: Two people can do it in fifteen.
20: You are starting to see the point of other people.
--
21: At four, the suits cut the shutter.
22: You drop the bucket one last time, straight into the lot.
23: Thirty clocks, all set for 4:10.
24: It is the loudest thing this city has heard in a month.
25: Every dead thing for a mile turns toward it.
26: The suits do the smart thing.
27: They drop their tools and run.
28: The noise goes with them.
29: So does the crowd.
30: By sunrise, the lot is empty.
--
31: That night, the knocking comes softer.
32: Then it stops.
33: You swore never to open that door unless there was no other way.
34: Now there is none.
--
35: Day 28.
36: You open the walk-in freezer.
37: Frost pours out across the floor.
38: Rhea sits against the back wall, pale, alive, her eyes open.
39: How long, she whispers.
40: You tell her.
41: She looks past you at the dark store, and the intercom crackles above you.
42: Attention shoppers, says Rhea's own voice, recorded on night one.
43: Do not turn the power back on.
--
44: Outside, the sun is rising.
45: The temperature is rising with it.
