## Chapter 1: The Night Shift (0:00 to 2:05)

Automatic doors open for anything that walks up to them. They do not check for a pulse. It is 2:14 in the morning. You are stacking cans in aisle 7 and the front doors have just slid open. The man in the doorway comes in every night at 2. Milk and cigarettes. Tonight, he just stands there. The doors try to close on him and open and close. Dana, the night manager, shoves him back outside with a shopping cart and locks the doors. He walks into the glass, backs up, does it again. In the office, there are 16 security cameras. On every screen, somebody is running.

Dana grabs her car keys. Her kids are across town. 1 hour, she says. Lock it behind me. It is the last voice you will hear for 14 days. So now it is just you.

40,000 products and a front wall made of glass. First problem, the glass. There is a steel shutter. It is electric. This store never closes, so it comes down once a year on Christmas Day. If the power dies first, you lower it by hand, one crank at a time with an audience. So you bring it down. Now it takes 40 seconds and it sounds like a freight train. New store policy. Nobody gets in. Then you do what you do every night. You count stock. Aisle 7. 9,000 cans. At four a day. That is 6 years from one aisle. There is enough food in this building to last a lifetime.

Reaching day 30 is going to be the hard part because food is not the problem. At 4:51, the power dies. The freezers stop humming. And at the back of the store, something goes clunk. The fire exit is held shut by a magnet. And a magnet needs power. You run the whole length of the store.

## Chapter 2: The Cold Chain (2:05 to 3:56)

The fire exit is already drifting open. You pull it shut. It will not stay shut. So, you fetch the pallet jack. A pallet of cat litter weighs almost a ton. You park three of them against the door. Next, the staff entrance. Six people have a key to it. You put yours in the lock and you snap it off. Now, nobody has one. If Dana comes back, she can knock.

Emergency lights are built to last 90 minutes. Long enough for everybody to leave. Nobody planned for somebody staying. When they fade, you learn something about supermarkets. They have no windows. With the shutter down, you have built a cave.

Aisle 12. Flashlights, batteries. You count those, too. Second problem, the rot. Without power, a fridge keeps food safe for about 4 hours. A full freezer about 2 days. After that, you are sealed inside a building with 4 tons of meat, milk, and fish. But there is a walk-in freezer, a steel room with a rubber seal built so that no air gets in. It works just as well the other way. It takes you 11 hours with one cart. You keep back the bread and the fruit. For one week, you eat like a king. Then you tape the seams and you write on the door, "New store policy. Nobody opens this ever."

On the third morning, you climbed to the roof. Two nights ago, the parking lot was empty. Now there are 200 people in it. Standing facing the doors. They are not hunting you. They do not know you are here. It is Saturday morning. They have come to do their shopping.

## Chapter 3: The Regulars (3:56 to 5:39)

Here is the thing nobody tells you about the dead. They don't hunt. They repeat. They arrive around 8. They lean on the shutter all day. Around 10 at night, they wander off. They shop when they always shopped. Nights are quiet. Nights were always quiet. That is why you took this job. New policy. You work nights. At night, the roof is yours. You cook up there on a camping stove from aisle 19. Never indoors. In a sealed building, a charcoal grill will kill you more quietly than they will.

On day five, the faucets run dry. No power, no pumps. Drinking is easy. You have six pallets of bottled water. The toilets are the problem. So, one bucket, one trash bag, and aisle 14, cat litter. You already knew it was useful.

On day six, a car pulls into the staff parking. It stops perfectly between the lines. It is Dana's. She sits behind the wheel with the engine running until the tank is empty. She does not get out. On day nine, the driver's door is open and the seat is empty.

On day 10, you are asleep on a pile of dog beds when the ceiling turns white. Every light, every freezer, and from every speaker in the building, the store playlist at full volume. The power is back. Outside, 300 heads turn at once. The main breaker is in the back

## Chapter 4: Forty Seconds (5:39 to 6:52)

office. Gray box, red handle, 90 yards away. You have never run this fast in your life. 40 seconds, then dark, then silence. But for 40 seconds, you were the brightest, loudest building in the city. New policy. The breaker stays off.

Too late. They are not leaning now. They are pushing. Third problem, the shutter. It was built to stop one man with a crowbar, not 300 people. By midnight, the steel has started to bow. So, you build a second wall. Pallets of bottled water, a ton each, parked against the steel. Then, shopping carts, nested, chained, 40 to a row. One row weighs as much as a small car. But a wall only buys time. You need them to leave. Aisle 12 sells alarm clocks, the loud kind with two bells. You set one for noon. Then in the dark, you throw it off the back of the roof as far as you can. At noon, it rings and 300 heads turn the other way.

## Chapter 5: Customers (6:52 to 8:56)

It works for 3 days. Then on the 14th night, somebody knocks on the fire exit. Three knocks, a pause, three more. The dead do not knock. There are five of them. A pickup truck, a tow chain, and a very simple plan. They saw the lights on day 10. They know somebody is inside, and they know what is in here with you.

Fourth problem, the living. You cannot fight five people. But you understand something they don't. If they rip that shutter off, 300 dead walk in right behind them. And then nobody has a supermarket. So you don't open the door, you open the store. One rope, one bucket. Lowered off the back of the roof at 2:00 in the morning. The first 20 cans are free. After that, you trade. Batteries, fuel, medicine, news. You have worked retail for 6 years. You know how to handle difficult customers. Policy one amended. Nobody gets in, but the roof is open.

By day 18, there is a line. A woman with a baby, two old men with a wheelbarrow, and a girl on a bike who only ever asks for cat food. On the 19th night at 10:00, something scratches at the staff entrance. Metal on metal. Somebody is trying to fit a key into the lock. It is Dana. She is starting her shift, but the lock is full of your key. She tries for an hour. The next night, she is back. She is never late.

On day 22, the bucket comes back up with a note in it. Pencil, a kid's handwriting. The men with the truck are done trading. They are coming tonight. They want all of it. Please let us in. Policy one says nobody gets in.

## Chapter 6: Closing Time (8:56 to 10:34)

Policy one was written by somebody who thought the problem was the door. Aisle 20, ladders. You lower one off the roof. The girl climbs up. In her backpack, one very large orange cat.

Then you get ready for the truck. You cannot stop five people. But you know what can? Aisle 12. 40 alarm clocks. Winding them takes one person 40 minutes. It takes two people 20. You are starting to see the point of other people. At 3, the truck backs up to the shutter. While they drag out the chain, you lower the bucket one last time straight into the bed of their truck. 40 clocks, all set for 3:10. It is the loudest thing this city has heard in a month. Every dead thing for a mile turns toward it, toward the truck. The men do the smart thing. They drop the chain and they drive. And the noise goes with them. So does the crowd. By sunrise, the parking lot is empty.

Day 30. Inventory. People, two. Cats, 1. Cans, plenty. Customers, 31 a night. There is enough food in this building to last a lifetime. Alone, you would not have seen day 23. You know that now. And every night at 10:00, a key scratches at the staff entrance. Dana is starting her shift. She has never missed one. Neither have you.
